Nobody should go quiet without anyone knowing

The 90-second wait and a rebuilt score fix the four lockouts. They don't fix why it went unseen for five weeks: a responder's score sinks, their phone goes quiet, and nobody is told. This is what we would build, in the order we would build it, with the assumptions I made where I didn't know.

What each person would notice

Kip, handler · phase 2

Each card says how busy that responder is against their usual week, and when someone is slipping, says so in plain words.

  • "1 ping in 7 days, usually 11." Last 5 pings shown. Badge: Quiet, because 3 of the last 5 were missed.
  • The busy card says so too: "19 pings a week, usually 13."
  • "Send check-in": the text Kip sends today, logged. He acts within 24 hours. Nothing in routing changes.

Today: two cards that look like two products, and a handler texting a responder privately.

Meteor Mite, responder · phase 1, then 3

Phase 1 puts them back in the rotation. Phase 3 tells them what happened.

  • Phase 1: the wait is 90 seconds again, and their score is rebuilt from their own home-area history.
  • Phase 3: recent pings (taken, turned down, missed), three ways to reply to a check-in, a test ping, and a "Back in rotation" notice.

Today: three weeks of silence, no explanation, and a handler's guess.

Kip put it plainly: "Mite thinks Mite's been forgotten." Nobody on the customer side could see it, and nothing on screen helped. Wen's 2019 question was whether the score should ease back on its own. The answer here is that it should not stay invisible either way: when it moves, someone is told, and there is a way back.

How Quiet works

Raises3 or more of the responder's last 5 pings missed, any area, with at least 5 pings of history. Computed from the ping log, not from the score.
Clears4 or more of their last 5 pings taken. Automatic. Kip can add a "checked in" note but cannot dismiss it.
Why harder to clearStops it flapping. Clearing as soon as fewer than 3 are missed gave up to 3 episodes for one responder. Clearing at 4 takes of 5 gave none more than 1.
Stuck QuietAfter 14 days it becomes a line in the weekly report to Helen. Never cleared by time alone.
Roster alarm7-day missed-ping rate above 5%, or 25% of the roster Quiet at once, alerts Marcus Oyelaran. This fires first if a deploy breaks everyone, and would have fired on 12 Aug.

What we know, what I assumed, what we don't know

Solid
  • A miss costs the same as a decline and never decays. The four were locked out over about a week by new misses.
  • The rule flags all four, 0 to 5 days ahead, in a backtest.
  • Visitors took 0 of 186 out-of-area pings before 4.2.
Not proven
  • That responders want the responder screens. No responder has been interviewed.
  • That check-in replies, or Kip's outreach, change anything.
  • That delivery failures are a meaningful cause. Evidence leans towards behaviour and timing.

What it deliberately does not do

  • Revert 4.2, or let anyone change a rank by hand. Nobody sees the score.
  • Make misses free, which rewards never answering. A miss counts, but only against home-area pings, and it can be recovered.
  • Write to the Responder Availability Record, which Supply reads. Quiet is display only.
  • Add an out-of-area score yet (next release, after Wen's replay), or start mutual aid (Q4).
  • Touch identity. Everything comes from the ping log and capability tags.

Decisions for you, Helen

  1. The goal. Prevent responders silently falling out first, with the responder screens held back. Agree?
  2. The action. If Quiet appears on Kip's card tomorrow, is "get in touch within 24 hours" what we expect from him?
  3. Sequencing. Does the 90s wait plus the rebuilt score go out alongside the badge, or ahead of it? I recommend ahead, with the roster alarm.
  4. The threshold. 3 of 5, or 3 of 7 to catch dips like Nightwell's?
  5. Responders seeing their own outcomes. Comfortable with that when we get to phase 3?
