Lockout early warning

Every rule below was replayed day by day against every responder, including the five weeks before 4.2.

The proposed rule (3 or fewer takes in the last 10 pings, plus 7-day pings below 60% of normal) catches all four lockouts with no false alarms, but it fires on the day of the lockout or a day later. The volume condition only trips once the collapse is already happening, so the rule repeats the locked-out count instead of warning ahead of it.

Use a 7-day score drop instead. It caught all four, 3 to 7 days before each lockout. It had one false alarm before 4.2 and three after, from two responders who really were struggling.

Alert when a responder’s score falls by 0.3 or more over 7 days.
That is: 3 × (missed + turned down) − 2 × taken ≥ 8 in the last 7 days.

Negative warning means the rule fired after the lockout. Vesper is the hard case for every take-rate rule. She kept taking Old Town callouts from third in line until 17 Aug, so her last-10 take rate stayed at 5 of 10 until after her pings had already dried up. The score rule catches her because it counts how many pings went wrong in the week, not just the share.

  • The alert is an action trigger, not a headline number. Report only the count of open alerts under the locked-out line.
  • A responder moves from the alert list to the locked-out count once their pings collapse. Their score repair should start at the alert.
  • Ironvale and Nightwell, the two false alarms, were real near-misses: Riverside is the worst area for unanswered callouts, and Nightwell lost first place in Midtown for a week. A check-in for them would not have been wasted.

Limits

  • Only four lockouts to test against. Every threshold here is tuned on four cases, so treat 0.3 as a starting point and recheck it after the fix ships.
  • The score is rebuilt from the routing code’s rule (+0.08 per take, −0.12 per miss or turn-down). Wen’s stored scores would let the alert use the real number.
  • Normal ping volume is each responder’s average from 29 Jun to 11 Aug. Live, it should be a rolling 6-week average.
