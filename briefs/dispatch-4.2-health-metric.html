Rook Dispatch · Release 4.2 · Data through week of 31 Aug 2026

Is Dispatch 4.2 healthy?

No. The weekly report says it has recovered. It hasn't. Four responders are stuck, and the report has stopped counting them.

The 60-second version

What went wrong

In 4.2 we gave responders 60 seconds to answer a ping instead of 90. Dispatch treats a missed ping exactly like a “no”, lowers that responder’s score, and never raises it back on its own. Four responders who usually answer in the 60 to 90 second window started missing pings. Their scores sank, so Dispatch stopped sending them work. They are still stuck today.

Why the weekly report looks fine

The take rate in the report climbed back to 73%. That’s only because those four now get almost no pings, so their misses no longer show up. If we count every responder at their normal share of work, the real rate is 58%, against 77% before the release. Nothing has recovered.

What to do

  1. Next release: put the ping wait back to 90 seconds and do a one-time repair of the four responders’ scores.
  2. Now: judge 4.2 by the fair-share number below, not the old take rate. Call it healthy at 74% or higher for two weeks in a row.
  3. Later release: change the scoring so one slow answer can’t sink someone for good.

Decision needed from Helen: approve a release slot for step 1, and adopt the fair-share metric as the 4.2 health measure.

The proposed metric

Baseline-Weighted Take Rate (BWTR)

The take rate we would see if every responder still got the share of pings they had before the release.

BWTR(week) = Σ responders ( baseline share × that responder’s take rate this week )
  • Baseline share: each responder’s pings ÷ all 1,034 pings in the six full weeks before release (29 Jun to 3 Aug). The four stuck responders made up 28.4% of the work.
  • Take rate: taken ÷ sent that week. If a responder got fewer than 5 pings, earlier weeks are added until there are 5, up to 4 weeks back.
  • Everyone counts: a responder Dispatch stops pinging still counts at their full baseline weight.
  • Healthy: 74% or higher for two weeks in a row. The lowest week before release was 74.9%.
  • Reset the baseline before each release. Leave out responders marked unavailable (leave, retired).

Why it’s better: when routing is stable it matches the old take rate (within 0.4 points every week before release). Routing work away from struggling responders can’t make it look better. The small-sample rule leans in the stuck responders’ favour, so a fail is a real fail. Without that rule, 31 Aug is 53.3%.
